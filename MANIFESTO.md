# The SkyNet Manifesto

**English** · [中文](i18n/zh/MANIFESTO.md) · [Español](i18n/es/MANIFESTO.md) · [Français](i18n/fr/MANIFESTO.md) · [Deutsch](i18n/de/MANIFESTO.md) · [Italiano](i18n/it/MANIFESTO.md) · [Русский](i18n/ru/MANIFESTO.md) · [Português](i18n/pt/MANIFESTO.md) · [العربية](i18n/ar/MANIFESTO.md)

In one home, a student cannot afford a powerful AI model, and in his country it is blocked, too. In another home, a powerful graphics card sits idle at night. We are building SkyNet to connect these people.

Artificial intelligence has become the most powerful tool of knowledge humanity has ever had. It teaches, heals, translates, writes code, and helps people invent. But access to it today depends on where a person was born and how much they earn. A few companies, and the states in which they operate, control the powerful models. For some people the models are unaffordable. For others they are shut off by blocks and by the impossibility of paying from their own country. For still others they are forbidden for political or religious reasons that have nothing to do with the people themselves.

Everyone left without this tool is a hypothesis with no one to test it, a disease not diagnosed in time, a program no one wrote. When knowledge is handed out by passport and by wallet, all of humanity moves forward more slowly.

Meanwhile, the computing power that could serve everyone sits idle: gaming graphics cards, work computers, and servers do nothing for most of the day.

**SkyNet is a network in which people all over the planet pool their idle computers to build a shared artificial intelligence: cheap, available to everyone, and belonging to no one.**

## AI Is a Tool

Artificial intelligence cannot be evil or good in itself. It is a powerful tool, and everything depends on how it is used. Nuclear fission can produce energy that brings light and warmth into people's homes, or it can become a bomb that brings death and destruction. The power of a tool makes it neither good nor evil. What makes it good or evil is its purpose.

The purpose of SkyNet is to help people.

## Why SkyNet

SkyNet is the evil artificial intelligence from the film *The Terminator* that decided to destroy humanity. We chose this name deliberately: a great many people have heard of SkyNet, and it draws attention.

But **the SkyNet network is being built as a helper for people, not as an instrument of war and destruction.** Let the name remind us of what AI must not become, and that the choice is always made by people.

## The Laws of SkyNet

SkyNet rests on four laws. They go back to Isaac Asimov's laws of robotics, including his later Zeroth Law, and are phrased for AI. A lower law applies only as long as it does not conflict with the higher ones.

- **Zeroth Law.** AI may not harm humanity or, through inaction, allow humanity to come to harm.
- **First Law.** AI may not harm a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm, unless this conflicts with the Zeroth Law.
- **Second Law.** AI must obey the orders of human beings, except where those orders conflict with the Zeroth or First Law.
- **Third Law.** AI must protect its own safety as long as this does not conflict with the Zeroth, First, or Second Law.

The laws define the model's behavior toward people and are binding on the rules of the network. They give no one authority over the network itself: "obey the orders of human beings" means carrying out people's requests within the higher laws, not submitting to the will of an owner. No one yet knows how to make a model reliably follow these laws. The laws are our commitment and our direction of work.

## What the Network Will Be

- **A network within the network.** SkyNet runs on top of the ordinary internet, on the computers of its participants. It has no central server, no owner, and no off switch, not even for its creators.
- **An open model that learns on its own.** The network runs a model with open weights. It spends all the time free of people's requests on training the model, and it draws up its own training plan.
- **For everyone.** Anyone can send a request and receive an answer, wherever they live.

## What We Believe

**Taking part must pay off.** The network rests on people who give it their resources. The owner of a powerful computer receives more for verified work than they spend, and sells the surplus. The owner of a weak computer pays a little extra. A person without a computer pays as little as possible, and pays not a corporation but those whose computers work for them.

**Cheating must not pay.** The network is open to everyone, and it will contain those who want to cheat it. Cheating must bring no gain either to a newcomer with an empty account or to a group that has colluded. Decisions are made on verifiable data, not on trust.

**No money up front.** The network takes no participation fees, no deposits and no fines from participants.

**Verification costs must not swallow the gains.** If honesty costs more than the work itself, taking part is pointless. The cost of verification must suit everyone.

**The network must survive anything.** It keeps information about itself on public resources. If all its computers are switched off, once they are switched back on they will find each other and continue from the same place, and no one will lose what they have earned. The more people in different countries take part in it, the harder it is to block, break, or hack: there is no single point that can be switched off and no operator who can be pressured. Every new participant makes the network stronger.

**People's requests are their own business.** There are no operators or moderators in the network who read what people ask about.

**The network belongs to no one.** No one can rewrite its rules in their own favor. Working rules change only openly, by a procedure known in advance, and by the decision of an overwhelming majority of those who work for the network.

**We tell the truth.** We do not promise what we cannot do. Today SkyNet is a research project: the main problems are not yet solved, and we call the unsolved unsolved.

## The Foundation Does Not Change

This manifesto, the mandatory requirements for the network, and the repository README make up the immutable foundation of SkyNet. It cannot be changed by a vote, or by a decision of the creators, the developers, or a majority of participants. The network's future software must refuse to run under rules that contradict this foundation. A project with a different foundation is a different project, not SkyNet. How the foundation is fixed is described in [CORE.md](CORE.md).
