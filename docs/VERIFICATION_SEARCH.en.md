# Search for Verification Independent of the Collusion Share

**English** · [中文](../i18n/zh/VERIFICATION_SEARCH.md) · [Español](../i18n/es/VERIFICATION_SEARCH.md) · [Français](../i18n/fr/VERIFICATION_SEARCH.md) · [Deutsch](../i18n/de/VERIFICATION_SEARCH.md) · [Italiano](../i18n/it/VERIFICATION_SEARCH.md) · [Русский](VERIFICATION_SEARCH.md) · [Português](../i18n/pt/VERIFICATION_SEARCH.md) · [العربية](../i18n/ar/VERIFICATION_SEARCH.md)

Status as of 24 November 2026.

This document is the result of the model council for stage 4 of the roadmap ("Search for a guarantee independent of the collusion share", [ROADMAP](ROADMAP.en.md), section 4). The council was looking for verification that each node performs itself, so that the agreement or collusion of other verifiers decides nothing. It was sought for two types of work: a Qwen model response to a user request and a fine-tuning step.

**Document status.** This is the result of reasoning and calculation by formulas, not a measurement. No experiments with the model, network runs, proof systems or model checking were performed for it. Participants and critics ran only short arithmetic programs and small brute-force searches on a CPU; two such searches from the ChatGPT papers were repeated by two critics, and the control hashes matched. Numbers marked [calculation] were obtained from formulas using explicitly named parameters. Participants supplied the model and GPU parameters themselves; no paper checked them against the published configuration of Qwen3.5-4B or the model of about 27 billion parameters. **Only decisions D1–D25** in [ROADMAP](ROADMAP.en.md), section 3, are adopted decisions. Everything else here is the council's estimate. Where critics disagreed, it says "disputed".

**How to read.** Section 1 — what is sought and under what conditions. Section 2 — what has been proven impossible and which impossibility claims have been refuted. Sections 3 and 4 — analysis of mechanisms for the model response and for the training step, with the best candidate and its limitations. Section 5 — what remains unproven and which experiments and calculations will resolve it. Section 6 — questions for the owner. Section 7 — who participated.

**Brief summary.**

1. **Model response.** Verification independent of the collusion share exists only where the entire response is verified by the payer whose money is at stake. The best candidate is "PPS": the payer in one pass recomputes the request and the finished response in an exact integer profile, payment comes only from their prepayment, and a dispute over a proof of error is checked by every node. The calculated cost is 1,2–3,8% of generation time, but only when generating one response at a time [calculation]. The candidate does not cover a consumer without equipment and depends on an unmeasured bit-for-bit match.
2. **Training step.** No verification of the step itself within about 20%, independent of the collusion share, was found. All six papers and all three critics agree on this.
3. **The only surviving path for training** changes what is paid for: the network pays for a proven improvement of the model on an exam chosen after the update is fixed. Under decision D18, this path is being investigated as a candidate for a new unit of payment, but has not yet been adopted; calculations, an experiment and corrections found by the critics are needed.
4. **The deciding factors are experiment D5** (bit-for-bit match of integer Qwen3.5-4B on two GPUs and between two computation modes, time, quality) **and the calculations of the mini-agents** from section 5.3; the final decision on payment for training (D18) is made on their basis.

Source documents: decisions D1–D17 and stage 4 in [ROADMAP](ROADMAP.en.md); the adversary model and the formal statement of D1 in [THREAT_MODEL](THREAT_MODEL.en.md), section 1.5; Variants A and B in [CURRENT_DESIGN](CURRENT_DESIGN.en.md); [OPEN_QUESTIONS](OPEN_QUESTIONS.en.md); [PILOT_RESULTS](PILOT_RESULTS.en.md); the mandatory requirements [REQUIREMENTS](../i18n/ru/REQUIREMENTS.md). Participants also read the project's earlier studies on exact arithmetic, sumcheck/GKR, Brakedown and the cost of the witness for LoRA (documents `LORA_*` and `HONESTY_RESEARCH_GATE` from the research archive; they are not part of this repository). Participants did not yet have decisions D6–D17: they were adopted that same day. When reconciling, these decisions were applied to the papers' conclusions; where a decision changes the verdict, this is stated directly.

## 1. Problem statement

### 1.1. What is sought

A procedure is sought that a node executes itself using data that the coalition cannot forge: the task input, the pinned model, pinned rules and, possibly, a mathematical proof. The node's decision must not rely on signatures and verdicts of other nodes. Then the share of colluding verifiers `q`, including `q = 1`, does not affect the decision.

Two types of work:

- **(a) model response** by Qwen of order 4–27 billion parameters to a user request;
- **(b) fine-tuning step** (LoRA or full) on a given data batch.

### 1.2. Mandatory conditions

| Condition | What follows from it for verification |
|---|---|
| **D1** | The expected gain from any false claim is not greater than zero both against inaction (`Δ₀ ≤ 0`) and against honest work (`Δh ≤ 0`), for any coalition, including a newcomer with an empty account and any number of keys. **Both** inequalities are required ([THREAT_MODEL](THREAT_MODEL.en.md), section 1.5). A limited positive leakage such as `R·q^k` in Variant B is not acceptable |
| **D2** | Only a verifiable result is paid for. Operating time, declared capacity and powered-on equipment are not grounds. The method by which the performer obtained the correct result (cache, another machine) does not matter |
| **D4** | Verification is automatic, without voting; each node performs it itself according to the pinned fingerprint of the rules |
| **D6** | Harm is included in D1: a false response must not be given to the user as verified, a false step must not enter the model. The result is used only after verification independent of the collusion share, or is explicitly marked as unverified |
| **D7** | Verification independent of the collusion share is required. Weakening D1 to "while the colluding share is less than `q*`" is not allowed |
| **D8** | Only verifiers assigned by lottery to the task and the user for their own request may dispute. Without a deposit. Others report suspicion on the board without the right to stop payment |
| **D15** | Simple recomputation that can be copied is not paid for |
| **R5** | No deposits, contributions or fines. One can lose only not-yet-matured rewards ("pending amount" `H`) |
| **Price benchmarks** | The surcharge for verification is about 5% of the work for requests and about 20% for training. These are benchmarks, not confirmed requirements |

Related decisions: D9 (the training plan is derived by rule from the catalogue and exam results, credits are issued once per task content), D10 (the exchange takes place with a single ledger entry), D12 (windows are counted in the network's active time), D14 (the price of a dispute is determined by the cheapest second-layer network), D16 (an honest hardware failure is not punished), D17 (payments for requests in Variant B come only from users' payments).

Statistical sampling, trust in a hardware enclave and a small cryptographic error are assumptions under the requirements: they may be proposed, but must be stated explicitly.

## 2. Lower bounds: what has been proven impossible

### 2.1. Bounds confirmed by the critics

These are statements about **classes** of verification. None of them excludes all conceivable verification; no one has proved a general lower bound on cost for all algorithms.

1. **Indistinguishability.** If a node accepts a result only from messages of other nodes, then under collusion of all their authors there exists a false result that it will accept: the coalition will send the same signatures and fingerprints as for an honest result. At `q = 1` a node is protected only by what it checked itself. (claude1, lemma 1; claude2, 3.1; gpt2, 9.4; ds2, 6.1. All critics agree.)
2. **Scale of "every node itself".** If a change in the common state (issuance of credits, weight) must be protected for each of `N` full nodes, each of them verifies each such result, and the total cost is at least `N·v`, where `v` is the cost of one verification. To fit within 5% or 20%, at `N = 1000` one needs `v/W ≤ 5·10⁻⁵` or `2·10⁻⁴` of work [calculation]. Hence three ways to obtain independence from collusion: the single participant whose money is at stake decides; verification is compressed (a short proof or a small exam); the result does not change the common state at all. (claude1, lemma 2; claude2, 3.4; gpt1, §2; ds2, 6.1. All critics agree; the ChatGPT critic corrected ds2's formula to `1 + n·a` instead of `(1 + a)·n`.)
3. **Sampling cells without encoding.** If a node reads on average `s` out of `N` independently replaceable cells (layers, positions, sequences), there will be a cell that it checks with probability at most `s/N`; its replacement passes with probability at least `1 − s/N`. (claude2, 3.2; gpt2, 9.2; gpt1, 5.4; claude1, I2. The Claude critic noted a gap in rigor for a verifier whose coins depend on the trace root; the conclusion does not change.)
4. **Sampling verification and the performer's margin.** For a false claim `σ` with costs `c_σ` and pending amount `H`, D1 against inaction requires `π(σ) ≥ (R − c_σ)/(R + H)`, where `π` is the detection probability. For a cheap forgery and an empty account, `π ≈ 1` is required, that is, full verification. For an almost honest forgery, a verification share of at least `m/(1 + m)` is required at margin `m`. For a key with `H > 0` and verification of one block out of `L + 1`, one needs `H ≥ R·((L+1)/m_s − 1)`: 36R for 36 layers and 64R for 64 layers with one verifiable block [calculation]. Fresh randomness of the node makes `π` independent of collusion, but does not make it equal to one. (claude1, lemma 3; claude2, 3.11, obtained independently. All critics agree. The ChatGPT critic clarified: with mandatory verification of the output block, the threshold for one almost free attempt is 35R.)
5. **Fixed linear checks over a field.** For `t < n` pre-fixed linear checks there is a common nonzero kernel: they do not see an error from it. A fresh uniform vector chosen after fixing passes a fixed nonzero error with probability at most `1/p`. Over bounded integers the bound does not apply (see 2.2, item 3). (gpt1, 5.2; gpt2, 9.1; claude2, 3.5; ds2, 5.2; claude1, B4.)
6. **Zero error is equivalent to determinism.** Verification with zero false acceptances for any coins becomes deterministic by fixing the coins. (claude2, 3.6.)
7. **Shared randomness under full collusion.** At `q = 1`, randomness shared by all nodes is possible only from outside the network (an external beacon or Fiat–Shamir); exchange of commitments between SkyNet nodes does not help. Personal coins of different nodes give different decisions, and a single calculation does not result. (claude2, 3.8; gpt2, 6.1. The ChatGPT critic added: the beacon must be unbiased, available, and revealed strictly after final acceptance of the block; this is already attack 1.5 and N13 in [THREAT_MODEL](THREAT_MODEL.en.md).)
8. **Free retries.** If an attempt can be repeated almost for free (for example, randomness is derived from the hash of the claim itself), the probability of success grows with the number of attempts. Fiat–Shamir cannot be used for cell selection. (claude2, 3.3; ds2, 5.1.)
9. **A verifier that constructs the result itself.** If a node computes the entire state itself and reads the claim only for comparison, then, by removing the comparison, we obtain an honest performer with the same work. Hence such verification costs at least 100% of optimal work. Only verification with a hint can be cheap: a ready response, a trace or a proof. (gpt2, 9.5. The Claude and DeepSeek critics called this the best formulation of the general bound.)
10. **Winning-token certificate.** For a hidden state of the general position, one cannot certify the winner without obtaining information about every row of the vocabulary that can win. In the worst case the entire vocabulary is checked. (claude1, B6; gpt2, 9.3.)
11. **Private input.** An outside node without the request cannot verify the response unless there is a zero-knowledge proof. The ChatGPT critic clarified the bound: a hiding commitment together with such a proof could convey the required property, and the lemma does not exclude this. (claude2, 3.7; ds2, 6.2; gpt1, §1.)

### 2.2. Impossibility claims that the critics refuted

1. **"Literal D1 requires exactly zero error"** (ds1, F1). True only if an attempt costs nothing. If randomness is external and unpredictable, and each attempt requires a new fixing and costs `c_min > 0`, then `Δ₀ ≤ εR − c_min ≤ 0` for `ε ≤ c_min/R` (claude1, lemma 4; ds2, 5.1). ds1's own candidate also relies on a hash with error greater than zero. All three critics refuted it. Caveat of the ChatGPT critic: the project has not yet established a lower bound on the cost of the cheapest attempt for any coalition, so a small error is compatible with D1 only conditionally (question 2 in section 6).
2. **"Verification of a finished response costs +100% of time by definition"** (ds1, F2). False for a finished response: when verifying all positions in one pass, the weights are read once, not for each token. In terms of operations, verification is indeed equal to generation, but in terms of time, when generating one response at a time, it can be tens of times faster. In addition, ds1 compared incomparable weight profiles. All three critics refuted it.
3. **"Exact linear verification requires at least `N` forms and `N²` operations"** (ds1, F3). False twice: fresh coefficients after fixing give a pass probability of about `Q^(−s)`, and over bounded integers one form with bit packing `Σ y_i·B^i` suffices when `B > 2U` (gpt1, 5.3; gpt2, 6.2); the identity matrix reads coordinates in `O(N)`. All three critics refuted it.
4. **"The minimum verification traffic for a response equals the size of the computation trace"** (a conclusion from ds2's lemma 5.3). Verification of a finished response needs only the response tokens, while the verifier of a short proof reads the proof and the roots, not the data itself. All three critics refuted it.
5. **"Any honest generation must perform as many operations as one-pass verification"** (claude1, §4.1 and B5). This is an upper bound on the cost of verification, not a lower bound (the Claude critic), and it does not hold if the performer has a legitimate cache of the finished result (the ChatGPT critic).
6. **"Cheap exact verification is impossible without secret randomness or cryptography"** (claude2, 3.5). A counterexample is the exact normal equation of ridge regression over rational numbers, which is verified deterministically (the ChatGPT critic).

### 2.3. Disputed or conditional bounds

- **Exam size `n ≳ 2σ²·ln(1/ε)/δ²`** (claude2, 3.10). The Claude and DeepSeek critics use it as a working estimate. The ChatGPT critic showed that one cannot derive the required `n` for any distribution from a single variance: specified alternatives and an independent sampling unit are needed. **Disputed**: suitable as an estimate in the named model, not as a theorem.
- **Quantitative barrier of a full proof** (claude2, 3.9): a 5% budget leaves about 1–5, a 20% budget about 6–18 field operations per nonlinear cell, whereas known rounding constructions cost tens and softmax costs hundreds [calculation under assumed costs]. All critics agree that this is a bound on known constructions, not a theorem.

### 3.2. The best candidate for the model response and the critics' corrections

**Candidate.** The entire response is verified by the payer itself. If they have a GPU on which the model fits, it is PPS (claude1). If there is no GPU but there is a fast channel, it is M1 (ds2) with a fresh secret vector for each request. In both cases the paid request gives no issuance of credits or weight (P0; this is consistent with D17), and the dispute is decided by each node on a proof of error (I8). Why collusion does not help: the decision is made by the single participant whose money is at stake; they have the complete data — request, response and model; verification is full, not sampled.

**Corrections without which the candidate does not hold.**

1. **Response withholding.** The ledger must contain the ciphertext of the response itself, not only its fingerprint: about 2–4 KB per response (the Claude critic). This is consistent with D10: exchange with one ledger entry.
2. **False disputes.** A "payer = performer" coalition can create any number of small requests and file a dispute for each, which all nodes download and recompute. D8 limits the flow of disputes by the number of requests, but self-paid requests do not limit this. No paper gave a solution (section 5, task M9).
3. **Timeliness and censorship.** The payer must be online and manage to publish the proof within the window, and the ledger must accept it. Censorship of a block within the window rests on the honesty of the ledger's external validators. This is an assumption about collusion in a different set, and it must be stated explicitly. For the payer's phone it is violated most often. The window under D12 is counted in the network's active time.
4. **Binding the ends.** A dispute about the first layer requires binding to the original private request, a dispute about the last — to the paid ciphertext (the ChatGPT critic).
5. **Stopping.** The first reason for stopping must be checked. The condition "the last token is EOS or the limit is reached" passes a response appended after an early EOS (the ChatGPT critic).
6. **INT-canon arithmetic.** The product of attention probabilities and values in the specification accumulates in int64. If it is not executed on tensor cores, the cost of the long 2000/1000 scenario grows from 3,8% to about 8% (the Claude critic's estimate); the solution is to split the 16-bit probability into two 8-bit halves. The ChatGPT critic found three more gaps: with one allowed key, softmax gives the value 65 536, which does not fit in 16 bits; the sum of squares in RMSNorm may overflow int64 (three components of `2³¹−1` already give more than `INT64_MAX`); explicit widths, RoPE positions and rounding order are needed. The principle itself withstands these fixes.
7. **Streaming output.** Under D6 the user must not see unverified text as verified. With streaming output, each shown prefix must be verified, and the frequency of such verifications is included in the cost (gpt2, §1).

**Limitations of the candidate.**

- The cost of 1,2–3,8% is correct only when generating one response at a time. It is advantageous for the performer to combine requests into batches, and with a batch of 8–32 the honest comparison gives 8–48% [calculation].
- The candidate does not cover a consumer without equipment. For 27B the payer needs at least 13 GB of video memory. The main consumer under the requirements is a participant without equipment. Under D6 their response can be issued only with the mark "not verified"; no paper has solved how to pay the performer for such a response without violating D1.
- Everything rests on the unmeasured assumption A1: a bit-for-bit match on different GPUs **and** between pointwise decoding and a pass over the whole sequence on one card. If Qwen3.5 has recurrent linear-attention layers, their parallel form rounds differently, and the cost of verification may grow by about a factor of two (claude1, 3.4, estimate assuming 10 μs per step).
- A dispute reveals the request: the block input almost reversibly reconstructs the text. There is no dispute without disclosure.
- Energy has not been measured: when limited by computation, the GPU consumes more than when limited by memory, so the share by energy may be greater than the share by time.
- Accelerators (interval certificate, winning certificate for greedy selection, cache of one's own prefixes) are permissible only on top of full verification, not instead of it.

### 4.2. The best candidate for the training step and its limitations

**There is no verification of the step itself within 20%, independent of the collusion share.** This is a general negative result, confirmed from different sides: the scale `N·v`, a trace of 64 GB–2 TB per step, proofs tens to hundreds of times more expensive than the work, the budget of elementwise operations.

With an **unchanged basis of payment**, two paths remain: full repetition of the transition (reference, +100% per node) and, long-term, a mandatory proof of the whole step (T3). T3 needs a concrete graph, a proven soundness of the composition and a complete estimate; currently there is none.

With a **change in the basis of payment** (owner's decision, section 6, question 1), the best candidate is a combination of T4 and ODU with the critics' corrections:

1. The exam is selected by an external beacon strictly after all updates are fixed and the block is finally accepted.
2. The threshold is taken from a statistical bound with a cryptographically small `ε` (as in T4), not 10%.
3. The exam is verified by a proof (ODU framework), so that the cost does not grow with the number of nodes.
4. Losses are limited by the arithmetic itself (the minimum probability in the profile) and are not clipped, otherwise harmful updates are paid for.
5. Attribution of duplicates must be solved without a sequential chain of proofs. No paper has solved this.

**Limitations.**

- This is not verification of the step. The exam does not see optimizer moments, does not certify the origin of the update and does not exclude training on the exam itself.
- Under D6 a false step must not enter the model. Improvement on the exam does not prove the absence of harm on rare requests and backdoors (Q4 in [OPEN_QUESTIONS](OPEN_QUESTIONS.en.md)). The candidate limits monetary gain, but not harm.
- It is unknown whether the honest improvement of one update exceeds the threshold: the noise of the loss difference `σ` has not been measured. If it does not exceed, honest participants receive no payment, and R6 is violated.
- Weak devices with a small update do not pass the threshold; "all free capacity learns" holds only as "learns", not as "receives payment".
- Relation to D9: the training plan is already derived from exam results. This makes the exam part of the core, but does not resolve the question of whether one can pay for improvement instead of the step.

## 5. What has not been proven and how to check it

### 5.1. What has not been proven

1. A bit-for-bit match of the integer profile on different GPUs and between pointwise decoding and a pass over the whole sequence on one card (A1, question Q2). PPS, M1, the interval certificate and any exact repetition depend on this.
2. The time of verifying a finished response relative to generation on real cards with batches 1, 8, 32; energy; the behavior of hybrid linear-attention layers.
3. The quality and speed of the integer Qwen3.5-4B profile relative to the usual one.
4. The cost of the interval certificate and the winning-token certificate on real weights.
5. The availability of dispute data, the limit on false disputes without deposits, the timeliness and censorship of the ledger.
6. For payment for improvement: the magnitude of honest improvement against the threshold, an attribution rule without a gain for duplicates, a loss bound without clipping, the cost of the exam proof.
7. For a short proof of the step: a complete estimate of one step and an end-to-end error bound.
8. The exact configuration of Qwen3.5-4B and the model of about 27B: all numbers in the papers were supplied manually.

## 6. Decisions required by this stage

The council does not make decisions. Below are the questions with the council's recommendation. The owner instructed the accompanying person to decide them; decisions D18–D25 were adopted on 24 November 2026 and recorded in [ROADMAP](ROADMAP.en.md), section 3. Each decision is stated after the question.

1. **Should the basis of payment for training be changed to "payment for proven improvement"?** Under D2 a verifiable result is paid for; improvement on an exam is formally one, but it is a different subject from the completed step. Three papers (claude1, claude2, ds1) consider it the main candidate, three (gpt1, gpt2, ds2) — another contract, not verification of the step. *Council recommendation:* allow this path to be investigated as a candidate for a new unit of payment for training, without calling it verification of the step. Decide finally after calculations M7, M8, M10–M12 and experiment O6. Conditions: an external beacon after fixing, a cryptographically small `ε`, losses without clipping, an exam proof, an attribution rule without a gain for duplicates. Under D6 record that improvement does not prove the absence of harm. **Decided — D18:** the path is investigated as a candidate for a new unit of payment for training, but is not adopted and is not called verification of the step. The decision on adoption — after M7, M8, M10–M12 and experiment O6 (it is outside D5 and requires the owner's authorization). The model is issued only by the rule of D9. The search for verification of the step itself (T3) continues.
2. **Is a small cryptographic error admissible?** The requirements call it an assumption. The papers claude1, claude2, ds2 and the Claude and DeepSeek critics believe that an error of `2⁻⁴⁰…2⁻¹²⁸` is compatible with D1 as an expectation if randomness is external and unpredictable and each attempt costs more than `ε·R`. The papers gpt1, gpt2 and the ChatGPT critic: one small error is not enough for D1; a proven lower bound on the cost of the cheapest attempt is needed. ds1 believes exactly zero is needed. *Council recommendation:* adopt a small error as an explicitly stated assumption under three conditions — an external beacon strictly after final acceptance of the block, a ban on Fiat–Shamir for sampling, a recorded lower bound on the cost of an attempt. Without this decision, both payment for improvement and short proofs are impossible. **Decided — D19:** a small error is allowed as an explicit assumption under these three conditions, not more than `2⁻⁸⁰` per attempt and with `ε·R` less than the recorded lower bound on the cost of an attempt. Ordinary sampled verification with error `q^k` is not permitted by this.
3. **Which external beacon and whose trust are admissible?** Without an external source of shared randomness at `q = 1`, no scheme with sampling after fixing works (bound 2.1, item 7). The sources have not yet been chosen (attack 1.5 and N13). *Council recommendation:* choose several independent sources and record trust in them as an assumption. **Decided — D20:** at least two independent public sources, the value is taken at a pre-appointed moment after final fixing, without the right to skip. Specific sources and the cost of influencing them — at stage 2. They are not included until a scheme with sampling is chosen.
4. **Should it be accepted that for the model response, verification independent of collusion exists only for the payer who verifies itself?** *Council recommendation:* yes. For a consumer without equipment, the response is issued with the mark "not verified" (D6 allows this), and delegation of verification is their own assumption. Separately decide how to pay the performer for such a response without violating D1: there is no solution. **Decided — D21:** yes. The network does not pay for a response without verification. A user without equipment can appoint a verifier themselves; this is their own assumption, and the network does not promise D1 for them. The D1 guarantee for such a user remains an open question.
5. **Should product changes be allowed for cheap exact verification?** This includes canonical token selection via Gumbel-max (changes the distribution compared to ordinary top-p/top-k sampling), an exact integer profile (may worsen quality), tasks with a verifiable outcome (formulas, tests) and new optimizers with an exact certificate (Gauss–Newton, ridge regression). *Council recommendation:* allow as separate task types of the plan with an explicit contract, after experiments O5 and calculation M17. **Decided — D22:** allow as separate task types of the plan with an explicit contract, after O5 and M17. Ordinary sampling remains available with the mark "not verified".
6. **How to limit the payer's false disputes without deposits?** D8 limits disputes by the number of requests, but a coalition paying requests to itself bypasses this limit. *Council recommendation:* calculate the cost of a false dispute (M9) before deciding; the council did not find a rule that does not require a deposit. **Decided — D23:** first the M9 calculation. PPS is not included until a rule limiting false disputes without a deposit has been chosen for it.
7. **Should the response ciphertext be stored in the ledger?** Without this, the performer can withhold the response. *Council recommendation:* yes, about 2–4 KB per response; this is consistent with D10. **Decided — D24:** yes, the ciphertext is recorded in the ledger in one entry with acceptance.
8. **Is the two-sided D1 statement also needed for partial forgery?** The claude2 paper asked whether a forgery that is worse than honest work but better than inaction is admissible. The formal statement of D1 in [THREAT_MODEL](THREAT_MODEL.en.md), section 1.5, already requires both inequalities. *Council recommendation:* do not change; therefore VYa-S and PVYa in the relative variant were rejected. **Decided — D25:** do not change.
